A new browser for Windows, now in beta

Browse like you think.

Thirty tabs in a strip, each one a sliver of a favicon. Panes keeps your tabs down the side, and one key lays every one of them out as a card on a canvas, where you can see, sort, scribble on and come back to all of them.

Download the beta

For Windows 10 and 11. It's an early beta, so expect the odd bug.

Coming from Chrome or Edge? Bring everything over in one go.

Go on, move one. It'll stay where you leave it.

Spaces

A place for each part of your day.

Work, home, the thesis. Each Space keeps its own tabs, its own colour and, if you like, its own logins, so the two halves of your life can be signed in to the same site as different people. Ctrl Alt 1 to 9 hops between them.

The canvas

Think right on the page.

Ctrl Shift K turns your tabs into cards on one board, grouped by topic. Write notes beside them, draw arrows between them, and open any card to use it live.

Overview

See everything at once.

Pinch or scroll out and the whole board fits the window, every section named. Click one and you're back in, right where it is.

Live cards

It keeps playing.

A card isn't a screenshot. The video carries on as you move around the board, and opens to fill the window without skipping a beat. Long ones can float off into picture in picture.

Find anything

Half a word is enough.

Type a few letters and Panes finishes the site name, then offers searches, your history and the tabs you already have open, so you go back to a page instead of opening it twice.

Right where you left it

Quit. Reopen. All there.

Close Panes, restart the computer, come back next week. Your tabs, cards and notes are there, and Ctrl Shift T brings back the ones you closed.

Split view

Split it any way you like.

Drop a tab on the page, or press Ctrl \. Drag the divider to give either side the room it needs. Every pane stays live.

Private by design

Nobody's business.

Ads and trackers are blocked from the first page, with no extension to install. Open a private tab when you want one, or a Tor tab when you want more, right beside the rest.

The assistant

Just ask.

Ctrl Shift L opens a chat that reads the page you're on. Ask it to find something, or to go and do it: it opens the sites and works through the steps.

It stops and asks before anything that matters, like paying.

Tidy

Sorted by what it's about.

One click groups the canvas by meaning. Recipes find recipes, flights find flights, and every group gets a name you'd have picked yourself.

Your model, your call

On device. Or bring your own.

The assistant can run entirely on this computer. Or plug in ChatGPT, Gemini, OpenRouter or a server of your own. Panes works fine with none of them.

Every tab you have open, laid out where you can see it, sort it and think next to it.

  • Every tab is a card Ctrl Shift K

    One key turns the sidebar into a board. Each card shows its page as you left it.

  • Grouped by topic

    Cards land in named sections, and you can drag them anywhere you like after that.

  • Notes and ink

    Write on the board beside your pages, draw arrows, circle things. It's your desk, not a list.

  • Live, not a picture

    Open a card to use the page for real. A video keeps playing as you move around the board.

  • See it all

    Pull out and the whole board fits the window, every section named. Click one to dive back in.

  • Find a card as you type Ctrl F

    On the board, start typing and the matching cards light up.

  • Tidy by meaning

    Let the assistant regroup the board by what each page is about, and name the groups.

  • Kept on disk

    Quit and reopen, and every card, note and section is back at once.

Tabs down the side, with titles you can read, and as many pages on screen as you need.

  • Vertical tabs

    Drag to reorder. Fold the whole strip away with Ctrl B and it peeks out when you need it.

  • Flick through Ctrl Tab

    Fly through your tabs without reaching for the mouse.

  • Split view Ctrl \

    Drop a tab on the page to put two side by side, or more. Drag the divider to resize.

  • All tabs at once Ctrl Shift A

    Every open tab in one grid, to pick from or close in bulk.

  • Groups Ctrl G

    Put related tabs under one name and colour, and fold them away together.

  • Closed by mistake Ctrl Shift T

    Brings back the tabs you closed, even after a restart.

  • Quiet tabs sleep

    Tabs you haven't touched in a while let go of their memory, and wake when you click them.

  • A tab that only downloads

    closes itself once the file has started, so you're not left with a blank one.

Separate places for separate parts of life, each with its own tabs and its own look.

  • Tabs that stay put

    Each Space keeps its own tabs and groups. Switching never mixes them up.

  • Its own colour

    The whole window takes on the Space's colour, so you always know where you are.

  • Separate logins, if you want

    Be signed in to the same site as two different people, one in each Space.

  • Hop between them Ctrl Alt 1–9

    Or click a Space in the sidebar. Right-click one to rename it or change its colour.

  • A board per Space

    The canvas shows the Space you're in, so work stays on the work board.

An assistant that knows what's on the page, and can run on your own computer.

  • Ask about the page Ctrl Shift L

    The chat reads the page you're on, so you can ask what it says without copying a thing.

  • Ask it to do it

    It opens the sites and works through the steps for you, and asks first before anything that matters.

  • Tidy a page

    Ask for just the recipe, just the table or just the steps, and the page is cut down to that.

  • Group the board by meaning

    One click sorts your cards into named sections by what each page is about.

  • On this computer

    Run a model locally, and nothing you ask leaves the machine.

  • Or bring your own

    ChatGPT, Gemini, OpenRouter, or a server of your own. Or switch it all off.

The web, without the people watching it. Most of this is on before you change a setting.

  • Ads blocked, built in

    uBlock Origin's and EasyList's lists, refreshed every day, with no extension to install.

  • Private tabs Ctrl Shift N

    Nothing saved, nothing remembered, sitting right beside your ordinary tabs.

  • Tor tabs

    When private isn't enough, open a tab that goes through the Tor network.

  • You decide, per site

    Location, camera, microphone and notifications are asked for once, and can be taken back any time from the site panel.

  • Kept on your computer

    History, bookmarks and settings live on your machine. There's no account to make.

  • One process per tab

    A page that misbehaves stays in its own box and can't take the others down.

The things a browser has to get right, done with the same care as the rest.

  • Reader mode Ctrl Shift E

    Just the words and pictures, set for reading.

  • Passwords and passkeys

    Saved and filled in for you, with a guided import from your old browser.

  • Picture in picture

    Long videos keep playing in a corner while you get on with it.

  • Find in page Ctrl F

    Quick, with every match counted.

  • Downloads Ctrl J

    Files land in a bin at the bottom right, ready to open or drag out.

  • History and bookmarks Ctrl H

    Proper pages of their own, not an afterthought. Ctrl D bookmarks the page.

  • PDFs and printing

    Open PDFs right in a tab, and print with a clear preview.

  • A tour that waits for you

    Five short stops, and you do each one yourself. Type "tour" in the address bar to take it again.

Built and tuned on Windows, for the touchpads, screens and habits people really have.

  • Touchpad gestures

    Swipe with two fingers to go back and forward, and pinch the canvas in and out.

  • Updates itself, in the background

    Most updates are a small download that's ready next time you open Panes. No installer to click through.

  • Starts fast

    The window is up and themed before the first page has finished loading. No white flash.

  • Easy on memory

    Tabs you aren't using let go of what they hold, so a long day doesn't slow the machine down.

  • Springy, not showy

    Everything moves on springs, quick to start and calm to settle.
